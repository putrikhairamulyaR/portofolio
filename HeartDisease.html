Heart Disease Prediction

Built and evaluated machine learning models to identify heart disease risk from clinical patient data and uncover meaningful risk patterns.

📊 Project Snapshot

👥 918 Patient Records
🩺 11 Clinical Features
🤖 3 Models Compared

⚠️ The Problem

Heart disease risk is influenced by multiple clinical indicators, making it difficult to identify meaningful patterns from individual variables alone.

The challenge was to transform patient-level clinical data into a reliable classification model while identifying which clinical indicators were most strongly associated with heart disease.

🎯 Objective

The analysis was designed to answer four key questions:

  • Which clinical and demographic factors are most strongly associated with heart disease?
  • How do combinations of clinical indicators distinguish patients with and without heart disease?
  • Which model among XGBoost, Random Forest, and KNN provides the best predictive performance?
  • Which model is most effective at identifying heart disease cases, particularly when minimizing missed cases is a priority?

🔎 Approach & Analysis

Data Quality Analysis
Reviewed 918 patient records and 11 clinical features, including missing values, duplicates, and invalid values.

Exploratory Data Analysis
Investigated demographic, symptom, heart-rate, and ECG-related patterns associated with heart disease.

Risk Factor Analysis
Examined Age, Chest Pain Type, MaxHR, Exercise Angina, Oldpeak, and ST Slope to identify meaningful risk patterns.

Combined Indicator Analysis
Evaluated how multiple clinical indicators together could provide clearer separation between patients with and without heart disease.

Predictive Modeling
Built and compared XGBoost, Random Forest, and KNN classification models using multiple evaluation metrics.

Model Evaluation
Compared Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-Score, and ROC-AUC to determine the most suitable model.

📌 Key Findings

Age Heart disease prevalence increased notably after age 50, with the highest prevalence at ages 55–65.
Chest Pain ASY chest pain showed the highest heart disease rate.
Exercise Angina Patients with exercise-induced angina showed higher heart disease prevalence.
MaxHR Heart disease patients consistently showed lower maximum heart rates.
ST Slope Flat and Down ST Slope were associated with higher heart disease prevalence.
Oldpeak Higher Oldpeak values were associated with increased heart disease prevalence.
Main Insight

Asymptomatic Chest Pain and ST Slope Are Strongest Indicators

The clinical data confirms that patients over 50 showing asymptomatic (ASY) chest pain, flat/downward ST Slopes, or higher Oldpeak values represent the highest risk categories for heart disease.

Key Result

XGBoost and KNN Outperformed Random Forest in Accuracy

Both XGBoost and KNN achieved the highest test accuracy of 89.67%, with XGBoost delivering the highest sensitivity/recall of 91.18%, making it the optimal model for clinical risk screening.

🚀 Impact

Turning Clinical Data into Predictive Insight

The project demonstrated how patient clinical data can be transformed into predictive insights for early risk screening. The analysis identified meaningful clinical patterns and established a model capable of identifying heart disease cases with 91.18% recall.
