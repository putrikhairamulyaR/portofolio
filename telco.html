Telco Customer Churn Analysis & Prediction

Diagnosed the behavioral and contractual drivers of customer churn, engineered predictive features, and built a machine learning model that flags at-risk customers before they leave.

📊 Project Snapshot

👥 7,043 Customer Records
⚙️ 33 Features
📉 26.5% Churn Rate

⚠️ The Problem

Churn quietly drains revenue in subscription businesses like telecom providers acquiring a new customer costs far more than keeping an existing one, yet the signals that predict who's about to leave are scattered across dozens of account and service attributes.

The challenge was to isolate which customer characteristics and behaviors most strongly predict churn, and turn those signals into a system that flags at-risk customers before they walk away.

🎯 Objective

The project was designed to answer the following questions:

  • Which customer characteristics and service usage patterns most strongly drive churn?
  • Does contract type and tenure outweigh pricing alone in predicting churn?
  • What reasons do customers most often cite for leaving?
  • Which machine learning model most reliably flags customers at risk of churning?

🔎 Approach & Analysis

1. Data Quality Analysis
Audited 7,043 customer records across 33 features checking missing values, duplicates, label consistency, and logical consistency between dependent service columns to guarantee clean inputs downstream.

2. Exploratory Data Analysis
Mapped churn distribution, geographic concentration, billing behavior, demographics, contract types, and tenure to pinpoint exactly where churn concentrates.

3. Churn Driver Analysis
Isolated the customer attributes with the strongest pull on retention, from tenure and contract type to service usage.

4. Correlation Analysis
Quantified relationships between encoded features and churn to rank the strongest predictive signals tenure, online security, tech support, and contract type led the pack.

5. Feature Engineering
Engineered business-driven features Total Services, Tenure Bucket, Charge Per Service, Billing Gap, and a High Risk Contract-Payment flag to sharpen the model's predictive edge.

6. Predictive Modeling
Benchmarked XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost, and Logistic Regression on Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-Score, and ROC-AUC, applying consistent threshold tuning across every model to find the sharpest churn predictor.

🚀 Churn Risk Dashboard

Of 7,043 customers, 1,869 churned 88.55% on month-to-month contracts, 57.30% paying by electronic check, 74.91% on paperless billing, and 55.48% within their first year. Phone Service was their most common subscription (91%), Fiber optic carried the highest churn among internet types, and 36% had a partner. Built in Power BI, the dashboard filters this exact segment live by contract, payment method, tenure, and internet type down to individual customer records.

Actionable Monitoring: Retention teams can pull the exact list of month-to-month, electronic-check, under-12-month customers on Fiber optic the segment driving most of the churn instead of reviewing accounts one by one.

📈 Results & Insights

Benchmarked four machine learning algorithms head-to-head XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost, and Logistic Regression to predict customer churn. The evaluation confirmed that Tenure, Contract Type, and Monthly Charges drive the bulk of churn risk.

Key Result

CatBoost and XGBoost Achieved the Strongest Predictive Performance

CatBoost and XGBoost delivered the strongest results, hitting an F1-Score of 0.638 with ROC-AUC near 0.85 reliably separating customers who will leave from those who will stay, even under class imbalance.

💡 Key Insights

Tenure Churned customers stuck around for a median of just 10 months a third of the 38-month median for retained customers.
Contract Type Month-to-month contracts churn at a markedly higher rate than one-year or two-year commitments.
Monthly Charges Churned customers paid more per month ($74.4) than those who stayed ($61.3) a sign of price sensitivity tied to perceived value.
Model Performance CatBoost and XGBoost outpaced LightGBM and Logistic Regression on F1-Score and ROC-AUC.
Main Insight

Customer Behavior Can Provide Strong Churn Signals

Churn can be predicted and pre-empted from tenure, contract type, and service usage alone, with service dissatisfaction and competitor offers standing out as the top reasons customers gave for leaving.

💭 Why It Matters

Retention shouldn't run on gut feel or reactive outreach after a customer has already decided to go. Pinpointing which characteristics drive churn turns retention into a data-driven, prioritized effort instead of a guessing game.

Comparing models also reveals which approach best converts these patterns into a reliable, actionable churn risk score for every customer.

🚀 Impact

From Customer Data to Churn Risk Scoring

The project turns raw customer data into a ranked list of who's about to leave and why, spotlighting tenure, contract type, and monthly charges as the levers that matter most.

Those insights power a system that flags and prioritizes at-risk customers by behavior, so retention teams can target outreach where it actually moves the needle.

With the top model hitting an F1-Score of 0.638 and ROC-AUC of 0.854, the approach proves it can reliably separate churners from loyal customers and feeds directly into the Power BI dashboard used to monitor churn day to day.
